# Military Organization Hierarchy

Most modern armies share a broadly similar hierarchical structure, though sizes and exact terminology vary by nation and era.

## Typical Army Hierarchy (Approximate Strengths)

| Level              | Typical Strength     | Commander Rank (approx)     | Constituent Units              | NATO Symbol / Notes |
|--------------------|----------------------|-----------------------------|-------------------------------|---------------------|
| Fireteam / Squad  | 4–13                 | Corporal / Sergeant        | —                            | Smallest tactical unit |
| Platoon           | 20–50                | Lieutenant                 | 3–4 squads                   | |
| Company / Battery / Troop | 100–250         | Captain / Major            | 3–5 platoons                 | |
| Battalion / Squadron | 300–1,200         | Lieutenant Colonel         | 3–6 companies + support      | Basic tactical unit |
| Regiment / Brigade | 1,000–5,000         | Colonel / Brigadier General| 2–5 battalions               | Brigade is more common modern maneuver unit |
| Division          | 6,000–25,000         | Major General              | 2–4 brigades + supporting arms | Combined arms |
| Corps             | 20,000–60,000+       | Lieutenant General         | 2+ divisions                 | Operational level |
| Field Army / Army Group | 100,000–1,000,000+ | General / Field Marshal   | Multiple corps / armies     | Theater level |

## Notes by Nation

- **United States**: Heavy use of Brigade Combat Teams (BCTs) as the primary deployable combined-arms unit. Divisions provide higher command.
- **United Kingdom**: Strong regimental identity; brigades and divisions form the deployable structure.
- **Russia / former Soviet**: Historically favored large divisions and armies; recent reforms emphasize battalion tactical groups (BTGs) and brigade structures.
- **China (PLA)**: Post-reform emphasis on combined-arms brigades within Theater Commands.

## Other Services

- **Navy**: Ships → Squadrons / Flotillas → Fleets. Carrier Strike Groups are a key US formation.
- **Air Force**: Squadrons → Groups / Wings → Air Forces / Numbered Air Forces.
- **Joint**: Modern operations are almost always joint (multi-service) and frequently combined (multi-national).

This hierarchy is a generalization. Actual strength, equipment, and doctrine differ significantly between nations and over time.
